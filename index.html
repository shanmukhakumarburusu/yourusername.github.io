Compact HF direction-finding loop array, 1 to 30 MHz

Summer research at NIT Calicut (May to Jul 2026), supervised by Dr. Karthik R. A Watson-Watt array of two crossed loops and a top-hat monopole, limited to a 10 m envelope.

The question

At 1 MHz a wavelength is 300 m, so a quarter-wave antenna would be 75 m tall. The project asked how much 360 degree bearing capability a 10 m passive structure can deliver across the whole HF band, and where physics stops it.

I simulated the bare structure with a single 50 ohm port, lossy aluminium and no matching network, so the results show the true reactive limits that a later matching network or DSP stage must be designed against.

What the solver showed

  • A parasitic resonance near 12 MHz where S11 approaches 0 dB.
  • Two better-matched regions, roughly 5 to 9 MHz and 18 to 25 MHz.
  • At 30 MHz the loop circumference equals one wavelength and the Figure-8 splinters into a cloverleaf, so clean-DF loops must stay below half a wavelength.

Next steps: tunable LC matching at the feed to recover 1 to 5 MHz, a fabricated aluminium-tube prototype measured in an anechoic chamber, a stacked array for NVIS elevation, and MUSIC or ESPRIT bearing estimation.

Dual-band 1x4 microstrip patch array

Aryatronix Pvt. Ltd. (Jul 2026). A corporate-fed array for 2.4 and 5.8 GHz RF counter-UAS detection. Results shown here are the 2.4 GHz band.

Problem and fix

  • Symptom: the far-field split into two lobes instead of one broadside beam.
  • Cause: non-uniform phase reaching the four patches.
  • Fix: a symmetric corporate feed giving uniform 0 degree phase at every element.
  • Isolation: a Defected Ground Structure under the feed to cut mutual coupling and surface-wave loss.
  • Tuning: three parametric sweeps to push return loss below -15 dB in both bands.
